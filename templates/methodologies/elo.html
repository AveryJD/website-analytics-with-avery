Elo Ratings

What Is Elo?

A team's win-loss record alone doesn't capture the strength of the opponents it played or how close those games actually were. Elo addresses that by giving every team a single rating that updates after each game based on how the actual result compared to what was expected, so a win over a strong team moves the rating more than a win over a weak one. Elo covers every regular-season game from 2009-2010 through 2025-2026. Because ratings update after every result, Elo reflects a team's current form and trajectory over the course of a season, rather than a single fixed-in-time snapshot.

How Ratings Update

Elo Calculation

Team Elo ratings update after every game of a season, starting every team at a rating of 1500 at the start of each season. Before a game, each team's expected score (between 0 and 1) is calculated from the ratings gap between the two teams using the standard logistic Elo expectation formula, where team A is the home team, \( R_A \) and \( R_B \) are the two teams' ratings, \( H = 30 \) is a fixed home-ice advantage added to the home team's rating, and \( D = 400 \) is the Elo scale factor that controls how quickly the expected score approaches 0 or 1 as the ratings gap grows. The away team's expected score is \( 1 - E_A \):

$$ E_A = \dfrac{1}{1 + 10^{\frac{R_B - (R_A + H)}{D}}} $$

After the game, the home team's rating shifts based on the difference between the actual result \( S_A \) and the expected score \( E_A \), scaled by a K-factor of 20 and a margin-of-victory multiplier \( M \), and the away team's rating moves by the same amount in the opposite direction:

$$ R_A' = R_A + K (S_A - E_A) \times M $$

The constants are:

ConstantValueWhat it is
Starting rating1500Every team's rating at the start of each season
Home-ice advantage (H)30Added to the home team's rating before the expected score is calculated
Scale (D)400How quickly the expected score approaches 0 or 1 as the ratings gap grows
K-factor (K)20How far a single result can move a rating

To get a feel for the scale, a team rated 100 points higher than its opponent has an expected score of about 0.64, and the 30-point home-ice advantage on its own is worth an expected score of about 0.54.

Scoring Results

Results aren't scored as a simple win/loss. Each result type is assigned a fixed value for \( S_A \), so a regulation win is worth full credit while overtime and shootout results are worth partial credit in both directions, and the rating system reflects how close a game actually was, not just who won:

Result Value
Regulation Win 1.0
Overtime Win 0.9
Shootout Win 0.7
Shootout Loss 0.3
Overtime Loss 0.1
Regulation Loss 0.0

The Margin-of-Victory Multiplier

The margin-of-victory multiplier scales each rating change by how lopsided the final score was, where the margin is the winner's goals minus the loser's. It grows with the goal margin on a log scale, so each extra goal counts for less than the one before. It is also dampened as the gap between the two teams' ratings widens, so a blowout win by the stronger team moves ratings less than the same margin from the weaker team. Shootout results always use a flat multiplier of 1.0 instead, since the shootout goal differential isn't a meaningful measure of game control.

The formula is adapted from Neil Paine's NHL Elo methodology, where \( R_w \) and \( R_l \) are the winning and losing team's ratings:

$$ M = \dfrac{(0.6686 \ln(\text{margin}) + 0.8048) \times 2.05}{(R_w - R_l) \times 0.001 + 2.05} $$

Limitations and Things to Keep in Mind

  • Hockey is noisy. Even a good Elo picks the winner only a little more often than always picking the home team: across all 20,382 games, picking the team with the higher Elo (home ice included) was right 56.5% of the time, against 54.2% for always picking the home team.
  • It only knows scores. Injuries, goalies, lineup changes, and rest aren't considered.
  • Empty-net goals inflate the margin. A late empty-net goal widens the final margin, so the winner's rating gain is larger than the game's closeness suggests.
  • Ratings reset every season. Every team starts at 1500, so early-season ratings sit close together and depend on opponents whose own ratings are still settling, and ratings aren't comparable from one season to the next.
  • Home ice is one fixed value. It is the same 30 points for every team and every season.
