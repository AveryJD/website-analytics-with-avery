Simple Rating System (SRS)

What Is SRS?

SRS gives every team one rating for a season: how many goals per game better or worse than an average team it was, after adjusting for the opponents it played and for home ice. Put simply, it is a team's goal differential per game, adjusted for who it played and where. It solves for a whole season's worth of results all at once, so the fit isn't sensitive to which order the games were played in. SRS is fit separately for every regular season from 2009-2010 through 2025-2026. For a rating that updates after every game instead, see Elo above.

How SRS Is Calculated

It estimates a single rating for every team, plus one shared home-ice advantage value \( H \), by minimizing the sum of squared residuals between each team's predicted and actual goal margins across every game in a season. A (home) team's predicted margin \( \hat{m}_h \) against an away team in any given game is simply the difference between the two teams' ratings, adjusted for home ice:

$$ \hat{m}_h = (R_h - R_a) + H $$

The optimization then solves for the full set of team ratings and \( H \) that minimize the total squared error between every predicted margin \( \hat{m}_g \) and the actual margin \( m_g \) across all \( n \) games in the season:

$$ \underset{R,\, H}{\arg\min} \sum_{g=1}^{n} (m_g - \hat{m}_g)^2 $$

The ratings are also constrained so that the league-wide average rating \( \bar{R} \) is zero.

The final output is a single rating per team. Taking the difference between any two teams' ratings and adding the home-ice advantage gives the model's predicted goal margin if those two teams played each other, with \( R_h \) as the home team and \( R_a \) as the away team. For example, a home team rated +0.40 against an away team rated -0.20 is predicted to win by 0.40 - (-0.20) + 0.26 = 0.86 goals, using a typical home-ice value of 0.26. The table below lists what goes into the fit and what it solves for:

PieceWhat it is
Game margin (input)The home team's goals minus the away team's goals in a regular-season game (a shootout win counts as a one-goal margin)
Team rating (solved for)One value per team, in goals per game above or below the league average
Home-ice advantage, H (solved for)One value shared by every team, fit alongside the ratings (it averages about 0.26 goals per game, and ranges from 0.13 to 0.36 across seasons)
Constraint (rule for the fit)The ratings average zero, so a rating only means something against the other teams in the same season

Limitations and Things to Keep in Mind

  • It measures goals, not shot quality. Good or bad luck in scoring and goaltending is part of a team's rating.
  • Every goal counts the same. Blowouts and empty-net goals count fully, and a shootout win counts as a one-goal margin.
  • One blowout can pull a rating a lot. The fit uses squared error with no cap on the goal margin, so a single lopsided game counts for more than several close ones.
  • It's one rating for the whole season. A team that changed a lot at the deadline still gets a single rating, so SRS can't show a trend.
  • Ratings compare teams within a season only. They average zero each season, and shortened seasons have fewer games behind each rating.
  • Home ice is one shared value. It is the same for every team within a season.
