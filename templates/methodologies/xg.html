Expected Goals (xG)

What Are Expected Goals?

Expected goals (xG) is the probability that a given shot becomes a goal. It captures shot quality, so a breakaway and a point shot are valued differently instead of both counting as one shot. A shot with an xG of 0.20, for example, goes in about once in every five tries, so a team that takes ten of them is expected to score about two goals. The model is trained entirely on shot-event data that I scrape myself, rather than relying on a third-party model. It covers every unblocked shot attempt (goals, shots on goal, and missed shots) from the 2009-2010 through 2025-2026 regular seasons: 1,726,876 shots and 117,224 goals. Blocked shots are left out because the coordinates recorded for them are where the shot was blocked, not where it was taken from. Overtime shots are included, but shootout attempts are not.

xG is the foundation for much of the rest of this page. RAPM uses the xG of every shot while a player is on the ice as the thing it tries to explain, and WAR builds on RAPM while also using xG to measure finishing (goals above xG), goaltending (goals saved above xG), and the value of drawing and taking penalties.

The Six Models

Instead of training one single model across every strength state, I train a separate gradient-boosted classifier (XGBoost, a decision-tree model that learns from past shots) for six different groups of similar situations, and I build that set of six models again for every season, so each season has its own six models. Each shot is used to train the model for the situation it was taken in.

Model Situations It Covers
5v5 5v5
Other Even Strength 4v4 and 3v3
Power Play 5v4, 5v3, 4v3, and any state where the shooter has more skaters
Penalty Kill 4v5, 3v5, 3v4, and any state where the shooter has fewer skaters

The Data

These are the shots the xG model works with, counted by the exact situation each was taken in. The shots-on-goal tabs count only goals and shots on goal, the unblocked-shots tabs add the missed shots, and the last two tabs show the shooting percentage (goals divided by shots) for each. In the tables, EN stands for empty net, and short misses (explained below) are not counted.

There are a few things to keep in mind with the data. Shots recorded as "short" misses (a miss reason added in 2023-2024, explained below) are much closer to fanned attempts than real shots, so I don't use them in the xG model. Three seasons were shortened, which shows up as low counts: 2012-2013 (lockout), 2019-2020 (COVID-19), and 2020-2021 (COVID-19). The "Other" column holds rare situations such as double-minor and matching-penalty combinations. 2009-2010's larger "Other" count comes mostly from shots whose strength could not be determined, because of gaps in the NHL's shift data for that season. 3v3 shots jump from 2015-2016 onward because that is when 3-on-3 overtime was introduced; the handful before then come from rare stretches of 3-on-3 in earlier seasons.

How It Is Trained

Each season's models are trained on the five seasons before it and not on the season being scored, so every shot is scored by a model that has not seen it. Training on the season being scored makes its xG more descriptive of that season, but the goal is a model that is predictive of the next season, so a model is never trained on the seasons it is used on. Five seasons is enough shots to learn from, without reaching so far back that it is learning an old style of play. The first five seasons in the data (2009-2010 through 2013-2014) don't have five earlier seasons to train on, so they share the model trained for 2014-2015, a simple way to cover the older data. That means those five seasons are in-sample (their shots were part of the model's training data).

All six models share the same XGBoost settings, and xG is never rescaled to match the number of goals scored, so every xG value is exactly what the model predicted.

One correction is applied before training. NHL arenas record shot locations a little differently, so each arena's shot distances are nudged toward the league-wide distribution for that season, by a weight of n / (n + 2000), where n is the number of shots in that arena-season. A small, noisy arena-season is left mostly alone and a large one is corrected almost fully. The 2000 is a hand-picked constant.

Penalty shots aren't ordinary shots, so they aren't part of the training data. Each is simply given the conversion rate of the penalty shots in the previous five seasons instead, so the value follows the current scoring environment.

Where Shots Are Worth the Most

The map below shows the average xG of a 5v5 shot from each spot between the red line and the end boards, using every out-of-sample season from 2014-2015 through 2025-2026 and leaving blank any spot with fewer than 10 shots. It shows what the model learned about geometry: xG climbs steadily toward the crease and is highest straight out in front of the net, which lines up with the shot danger zones.

One thing to keep in mind is the area behind the goal line, which looks dangerous on this map but in realit isn't nearly that dangerous. The NHL records a shot at the spot where the shooter was when it happened, so a wrap-around shot, which starts behind the net and ends up on the goalie from close range, is logged as a shot from behind the net, and the model rates those shots highly. Few shots are taken from back there, so a handful of goals can also make a spot look better than it is. In reality these shots score far less often than the model expects: across the out-of-sample seasons, 5v5 shots from behind the net scored about 430 goals against about 660 expected.

Model Performance

AUC (area under the ROC curve) measures how well the model separates goals from non-goals, where 0.5 is no better than random and 1.0 is perfect separation. Log loss measures how close the predicted probabilities are to what happened (lower is better), and calibration is total predicted xG divided by actual goals, where 1.00 means the model predicted exactly the right number of goals. Every season from 2014-2015 on is scored out of sample, and the shaded seasons mark the in-sample seasons. Limitations and Things to Keep in Mind

  • Power-play xG is the weakest model. Public play-by-play has no passing or screen information, and those are what make many power-play chances dangerous, so power-play AUC has slipped from about 0.73 in 2014-2015 to about 0.67 in 2025-2026, and its calibration has run high (up to 1.15) in recent seasons.
  • The small models are noisy. They train on only a few hundred to a few thousand shots a season, so penalty kill calibration, for example, ranges from 0.84 to 1.12 across seasons.
  • xG can't see passing or screens. A player who sets up good chances gets credit only through on-ice ratings, while the shooter who finishes them gets credit for beating xG, so scorers tend to rate a bit above playmakers.
  • The model doesn't know who is shooting or in net. It only knows which hand the shooter shoots with and the goalie catches with. That is deliberate, since finishing and goaltending are measured separately, but it means xG is the chance an average shooter would score against an average goalie.
  • Blocked shots aren't modeled, so a player or team who blocks shots well gets no xG credit for it.
  • Second shots in a scramble count in full. A flurry discount is used only for RAPM, so a player's own xG totals count every shot in a rebound chain at full value.
  • Penalty shots use a fixed value (per season) that doesn't vary by shooter, goalie, or the actual shot taken.
  • The prior miss reason changed in 2023-2024. It carries more detail in recent seasons than in older ones (see the features above).
  • The first five seasons are in-sample. Their xG, and anything built on it, is flattered a little, so treat 2009-2010 through 2013-2014 as a bit more descriptive than predictive.
  • Some NHL data is missing. Five games have no or partial shift data, so they are missing from, or only partly counted in, the on-ice stats, RAPM and WAR, though their shots and goals still count in individual stats. Three are in 2009-2010 and have none (PIT at CAR on 2009-10-14, NYI at DAL on 2010-01-08, and SJS at CBJ on 2010-02-10), one is in 2010-2011 and has about half (WSH at CAR on 2010-10-27), and one is in 2013-2014 and has none (CBJ at DAL on 2014-04-09). In 2009-2010, 2,161 shots (about 2.1% of the season's shots) have no strength state because of gaps in the shift data: 256 are from the three games above and the rest are spread over about 290 other games. They count in the all-situations stats but not in the 5v5, 5v4 or 4v5 splits, and the xG model scores them with its catch-all other even strength model, so that season's xG is less reliable. One game in 2009-2010 (TBL at FLA on 2010-01-16) has no faceoff data.
